# Ashen Banner Tactics Online

A turn based tactics game in the style of Fire Emblem that runs in a browser.
One HTML file, no install, no account. Open it on a phone, a tablet or a
computer and play. Up to six people can play the same battle across separate
devices.

Live at `casimbahadar.github.io/ashen-banner-tactics/`.

---

## What you can play

### The campaign
Eighteen chapters. The early ones are each built around one thing to learn:
armour that needs magic, fliers that need bows, a wall of resistance, a
healer, mounts, flight. Some of them are real gates, meaning a company without
the answer is genuinely stopped rather than merely slowed. Chapters 7 to 18
are fought on full maps made by the Fire Emblem community, with each map's
real terrain.

Nobody dies permanently. A soldier who falls is back for the next chapter,
keeps their experience, and can be rebuilt before the next march.

The last chapter remembers the prequel: once you have finished it, the throne
in chapter 18 is held by the monarch your story put there.

### The Fall of the Crown
The prequel, set nine years before the campaign. King Orvane is dying, his
last wish names his son, and the law names his daughter. You play Captain
Maren, sworn to the crown and a friend to both.

- **Forty chapters.** Eight shared chapters, then you choose a side: serve
  Queen Seryth, or follow Prince Caedmon. Each side is sixteen chapters of its
  own, and both end at the same gate.
- **Told in scenes.** Characters speak with animated portraits before, during
  and after battles, in the style of the handheld games.
- **Its own world map.** Every chapter has a place on the kingdom, with what
  is happening there and the history behind it.
- **Choices that are remembered.** How many herds, ships, wagons or fields you
  save changes who joins you, who fights beside you later, and even whether
  the armoury has stock.
- **Battles with more than two sides.** Third parties fight everyone, and
  every extra side has its own colour: the enemy is red, then yellow, orange
  and purple.
- **Chapters across two maps.** Wounds carry over, and anyone who falls sits
  the second map out.
- **Your company or the story's.** Play with the story's own soldiers, or
  bring your own: each chapter they fight at that chapter's level with the
  standard arms for their class, and your company itself is never changed.

How its battles are built:

- **Enemies come in waves.** The nearest group charges first and the next
  follows a couple of turns later, with the wave size growing as maps get
  bigger. Until its wave moves out, an enemy guards its ground and fights
  anyone who comes within reach.
- **Holders and healers.** Some enemies hold the bridges, gates and narrow
  passes that matter most on each map, and from chapter 5 enemy healers stay
  behind the line and mend their wounded.
- **Levels on the field.** Soldiers level up the moment they earn it, mid
  battle, and every stat rolls against its growth, as in the official games.

### Skirmish
One battle, scaled to your company.

### Score Run
One battle. Type a seed and everyone who types the same seed gets the same
map, the same enemies and the same six soldiers, so scores can be compared.
Combat rolls stay random. Points: a win is 1000, each kill 50, each turn taken
costs 20, each soldier lost costs 150.

### Gauntlet
Five battles that get harder, with one squad and no healing in between.
Damage and weapon wear carry over. Anyone who dies is gone for the rest of the
run. Experience earned counts for the run only.

### Split Field
Two to six squads on one map against a shared enemy army, all scored
separately. You can attack rival squads, but each rival killed costs you 100
points. When the enemies have a choice of equal targets they go for whoever
has damaged them most, then the weakest, then whichever squad the turn number
picks. That rule is stated on screen so nothing feels arbitrary.

### Hold the Point
A guarded fort in the middle. End your turn with a soldier alive on the fort
and you score a hold; three holds wins. Stepping off or losing that soldier
freezes your count until you take the fort back. At turn 15 the most holds
wins, then the higher score.

### Team Battle
Players against players with no computer army, in nine formats: 1v1, 1v2,
1v3, 1v4, 1v5, 2v2, 2v3, 2v4 and 3v3. Squads on the same side cannot hurt
each other, do not block each other's movement, and their scores add
together. Win by clearing the other side off the map or by leading at turn 20.

## Playing with other people

**On one device.** Split Field, Hold the Point and Team Battle can be passed
between people on a single phone. Each player moves only their own squad.

**Across devices.** Challenges, then Online. One player opens a room, picks
how many players it holds, and shares a six character code. Everyone else
types the code. The player who opened the room starts it whenever they like;
two is enough, you never have to fill the room. It works across any mix of
phones, tablets and computers.

Everyone sees the same map and the same dice, because the battle is played
from a shared list of moves rather than by sending pictures of the board
around.

There is a **Test the connection** button that checks the whole chain from one
device with nobody else involved. Worth running before showing the game to
anyone, since the server sleeps when unused and takes a moment to wake.

## Bringing your own squad

Any challenge can be fought with:

- **The standard six**, which keeps scores comparable between players.
- **Your own roster.**
- **Your roster under a budget**, 60 points at one per level.
- **A company code** somebody sent you.

Anything other than the standard six is scored on a separate board, so each
board only compares like with like.

**Company codes** turn your squad into a block of text anyone can paste.
Names, classes, levels, the stats they grew, what they carry and its
remaining uses all travel. Anything the receiving game cannot use is dropped
with a note rather than breaking the import, and edited codes cannot smuggle
in impossible levels or stats.

## Building your own campaigns

The builder is under Make.

**The world and the story.** Name your continent, list its countries, and pick
the kind of story you are telling. Each chapter then opens with a briefing
built from your world, and you can rewrite any sentence the game can use.

**Chapters.** Paint the map, place enemies, set the objective and the turn
limit, and choose which enemies can be recruited by talking to them.

- **Any size.** Type a width and a height, each from 5 to 40. Growing a map
  adds plain ground; shrinking it removes anything that falls off the edge
  and tells you what.
- **Finding the enemy you want.** Narrow the classes by weapon, by how they
  move and by whether they have promoted, or search by name, then pick from a
  grid that shows each class's figure.
- **Generated maps.** Lay out a fair outdoor or indoor map at the chosen size,
  then paint over anything you want to change.

**Maps and tiles.** Chapters can use a ready made map instead of a painted
one, and five community tilesets are built in for painting with real tiles.
Maps made by other people are added on the Maps screen and stored in your
browser with their own tiles, so the file is never needed again. A map
library kept beside the game lists what is available and downloads a map only
when you ask for it.

Campaigns can be shared as a code or downloaded as a file.

## Soldiers

Build them yourself or generate a company. There are 1,820 classes: 364 to
start in and 1,456 to promote into. Each combines weapon types from thirteen
kinds: sword, lance, axe, bow, dagger, staff, fire, wind, lightning, light,
dark, breath and beast.

Soldiers grow, form bonds with whoever fights beside them, get tired if worked
too hard, and can be rebuilt for free if a build turns out badly.

Portraits can be uploaded. Any size or shape works: the whole picture is
scaled to fit the portrait window, so nothing is cut off and nothing is
stretched.

## Maps and terrain

Generated maps follow rules taken from real Fire Emblem chapter maps: one
connected walkable field with no sealed pockets, no lone wall tiles, water as
proper bodies rather than straggling lines, woods and hills grown as clumps,
and clear ground at the edges for both sides to muster.

Community maps are read square by square from the tileset they were made
with, so every square plays as its real terrain. Cracked walls and doors can
be broken through, and some trees can be felled.

Tap any square in battle to be told what it is and what it does for you. Maps
larger than the screen scroll, and the board follows whoever you select.

## How this is built

One HTML file with everything inside it, including the tiles, the portraits
and both campaigns. No build step, no libraries, no framework. It runs from a
web address, from a file on your computer, and offline once loaded. Online
play is the only part that needs a connection.

**Testing.** Eighty test harnesses run the actual shipped code, not a copy of
it. Some notable ones:

- Two real browser clients play an online match through a stand in server and
  must keep identical boards, move for move.
- The battle is proved to depend on nothing but its own shared dice, by
  replaying the same match with the random generator pinned to opposite
  extremes and requiring identical output.
- The teaching chapters are played hundreds of times, with and without the
  answer they teach, to show each gate really stops a company that lacks it.
- Every chapter of the prequel is played by a test bot that records how many
  enemies come at you at once, how many soldiers fall in the first turns, and
  whether the turn limit is fair.
- Every line of the prequel's script is checked for unfilled gaps, unknown
  speakers and broken sentences.

**The server**, used only for online play, stores rooms and the list of moves.
It holds no accounts and no personal information. Every read and write goes
through a checked server function; the tables themselves are closed.
